Yes — Wireless Mic is 100% free and open-source under the MIT license. There are no ads, no subscriptions, no premium tiers, no in-app purchases, and no hidden costs. You get every feature for free, forever. You can even inspect and modify the source code yourself.
Every audio packet is encrypted with AES-256 CBC encryption before leaving your phone. The encryption key is derived from a random 4-digit PIN using SHA-256, and each packet gets a unique random Initialization Vector (IV). This means even if someone intercepts your network traffic, the audio is completely unreadable. Read our full security breakdown →
Absolutely. Wireless Mic registers as a standard Windows audio input device via VB-Audio Virtual Cable. Any application that accepts microphone input — including Zoom, Discord, OBS Studio, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Skype, Audacity, and hundreds more — will work seamlessly. See the VB-Cable configuration guide →
No. Wireless Mic works entirely over your local Wi-Fi network or USB tethering. Your audio data never touches the internet, never passes through any cloud server, and never leaves your local network. This also means it works in air-gapped or offline environments. Read our privacy policy →
Wireless Mic achieves approximately 20 milliseconds of latency using optimized UDP streaming — that is virtually imperceptible in real-time conversations, live streaming, podcasting, and gaming. For comparison, Bluetooth microphones typically have 100-200ms of latency. Technical details →
Wireless Mic supports Android 10.0 (API level 29) and higher. This covers the vast majority of Android devices currently in use. The app requires microphone permission and same-network connectivity. Check system requirements →
Windows 10 and Windows 11 (64-bit) are fully supported. The Windows client requires Python 3.10+ (bundled in the installer) and VB-Audio Virtual Cable (also included). Download the Windows client →
Yes. You can use USB tethering as an alternative to Wi-Fi. Simply connect your phone to your PC with a USB cable, enable USB tethering on your Android device, and use the tethering IP address in the app. USB provides an even more stable, dedicated connection. USB tethering guide →
